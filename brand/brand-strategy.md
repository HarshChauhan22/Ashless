# Brand Positioning — Top 3 Names

**Superseded 2026-09-16 — kept for history, not the current decision.** "Jeb" won this round and shipped as the app name for a while, but the project owner later ran a second naming round directly (English, smoke-themed, deliberately not Hindi-rooted) and picked **Ashless** instead — see `docs/project/DECISIONS.md`. This document is preserved as an accurate record of the first round's reasoning; it isn't rewritten to fit the new name, and none of its brand-voice/tone guidance is invalidated by the rename (that part still applies to Ashless too — see `visual-identity.md` and `app-store-branding.md` for what actually changed).

Context for all three: India-first smoking cessation app built around the Digital Smoking Room ritual-replacement mechanic. Brand must feel modern, supportive, motivating, trustworthy, emotionally intelligent, non-judgmental — never clinical, never preachy, never cute-for-kids.

---

## 1. Jeb

**Positioning statement**
For Indian smokers who want to quit without being lectured at, Jeb is the app that turns every craving into cash you keep — by recreating the moment you'd buy a cigarette and redirecting that money into your pocket instead of up in smoke.

**Tagline**
*Keep the money. Keep the win.*
(alt: *Your pocket. Not the packet.*)

**One-line description**
Jeb turns your next cigarette craving into money back in your pocket.

**Elevator pitch**
Every craving has a price tag — the cost of the cigarette you almost bought. Jeb gives that craving somewhere to go: open the app, walk through the exact ritual of "buying" your usual quantity, and instead of lighting up, that same money drops straight into your Quit Wallet. No willpower lectures, no shame, no health-class guilt trip — just a running, visible number that proves every craving you survived made you richer. Jeb is built for the Indian smoker's real day: paan shop runs, chai-break cigarettes, the ₹10-a-stick habit — and it scales the same emotional trick to any market, anywhere, because "money you almost spent" needs no translation.

**Brand personality**
The switched-on friend who's quit before and doesn't make it weird about it. Warm, a little cheeky, quietly proud of you, never a nag. Think: the friend who high-fives you, not the doctor who lectures you.

**Tone of voice**
- Direct and plain-spoken, short sentences.
- Celebrates money kept, not cigarettes avoided (reframes the win as a gain, not a deprivation).
- Uses "you" a lot, first person for milestones ("I kept ₹840 this week").
- Never says "addiction," "disease," "patient." Says "craving," "urge," "ritual," "win."
- Humor is allowed; guilt is not.

## 2. Rok

**Positioning statement**
For Indian smokers fighting the urge in real time, Rok is the app that gives the craving a stop button — turning the two minutes you'd spend buying a cigarette into two minutes that put money back in your hands.

**Tagline**
*Rok. Rakho. Repeat.* ("Stop. Keep. Repeat.")
(alt, English-first market: *Stop the urge. Start the savings.*)

**One-line description**
Rok is the two-minute stop button between you and your next cigarette.

**Elevator pitch**
The craving window is short — usually under five minutes. Rok is built entirely around that window: the second the urge hits, you open the app, it walks you through the exact ritual you'd normally do at the shop, and by the time you'd have lit up, the money's sitting in your wallet instead. The name does double duty — "rok" means stop in Hindi, and it sounds like a full stop in any language. It's built for the moment of maximum craving, not for retrospective willpower — a product for the 30 seconds where quitting actually happens.

**Brand personality**
The steady hand. Calm under pressure, a little bit martial-arts-instructor energy — composed, precise, in control when you're not. Less playful than Jeb, more focused.

**Tone of voice**
- Short, imperative sentences during the craving flow ("Breathe. Wait. Keep it.").
- Confident, not clinical — coach, not doctor.
- Data-forward outside the craving moment (streaks, ₹ saved, urges beaten) — proof over pep talk.
- No fear language, no countdown-to-death-style stats.

## 3. Instead

**Positioning statement**
For smokers anywhere who are tired of quitting apps that guilt them, Instead is the app built around one swap: instead of a cigarette, you keep the cash — turning willpower into a number that grows.

**Tagline**
*I wanted to smoke. I kept the money instead.*
(alt, shorter: *Do this instead.*)

**One-line description**
Instead replaces the cigarette you almost bought with the money you get to keep.

**Elevator pitch**
Every "quit smoking" app talks about what you're giving up. Instead talks about what you're getting: the exact cash you were about to spend, now sitting in an account with your name on it. The whole product is one sentence — "I wanted to smoke, but instead, I kept the money" — repeated back to you every time you win. It's built to travel: no Hindi required, no health jargon, just a universally understood swap that works in Mumbai or Manila.

**Brand personality**
The calm reframer. Doesn't fight the craving, redirects it. Understated, confident, slightly minimalist — feels more like a modern fintech app than a health app.

**Tone of voice**
- Present tense, first person, always completing the sentence "Instead, I ___."
- Minimal exclamation marks — confidence over hype.
- Frames every feature as a substitution, never a restriction ("swap," "redirect," "instead of" as recurring verbal device).
- Money language borrowed from savings apps (balance, streak, milestone), not health apps (progress, recovery, relapse).

---

## Final Recommendation

**#1 — Jeb.** It's the only name in the shortlist where the word *is* the mechanic: jeb means pocket, and the entire product is about money staying in your pocket instead of leaving it. That kind of literal-but-not-clinical fit is rare and worth more than a cleverer pun that needs explaining (Dum) or a safer word that means nothing product-specific (Unbound, Rewire). It's short, easy to say and type in any language, has no medical or childish register, lends itself to a genuinely ownable non-cliché symbol (the Kept Pocket), and — as far as I can tell without a formal search — isn't crowded by an existing well-known brand the way Unbound, Rewire, and Instead are. It reads equally well as an India-first name and as something that travels once the product expands beyond India, since "pocket" needs no translation.

**#2 — Rok.** Backup if Jeb runs into a trademark or App/Play Store namespace conflict on formal search. It's shorter still, carries its own clean meaning ("stop"), and is built for the exact moment the product cares about most — the live craving. It trades some of Jeb's warmth and money-specificity for sharper urgency; a good fit if the brand wants to lean more "craving interrupt tool" than "savings app."

**#3 — Instead.** Backup if the team wants to de-risk the Hindi-rootedness entirely for faster non-Indian expansion, or if user testing shows Jeb/Rok don't land with non-Hindi-speaking urban English-first Indian users. It has the strongest direct link to the brief's own emotional line ("I wanted to smoke, but instead...") and the best out-of-the-box global comprehension, but it's a common English word — expect real Play Store search crowding and a weaker trademark position unless the visual identity (logo, color, type) carries a lot of the distinctiveness on its own.

Before committing to any of the three: run an actual trademark class search (India Class 9/42 for the app, Class 44 if positioning touches health services), and check Play Store listing-name collisions, .com/.in domain availability, and Instagram/X handle availability. None of that has been verified here.
